Multimodal AI & foundation models

Models grounded in physical signals

IoT data are heterogeneous, noisy, and expensive to label. My work studies how knowledge can move between modalities, environments, and users without discarding the physics that make each signal meaningful.

I combine signal-processing structure with learned representations: public audio can supervise structural vibration, time-series foundation models can support environmental prediction, and structured activity representations can connect continuous sensor streams to language.

Technical lens

My methods span cross-modal alignment, CNNs, temporal convolutional networks, variational autoencoders, lightweight adapters, instruction-guided generation, domain adaptation, and task-specific evaluation. I treat model accuracy as one layer of a larger system: the representation must remain useful across people, homes, sensors, and downstream language generation.

Current direction

I am developing natural-language interfaces for continuous IoT data by integrating signal processing into tokenization and learned representations. The goal is grounded question answering that preserves temporal and physical context without making long-running sensor histories computationally intractable.
