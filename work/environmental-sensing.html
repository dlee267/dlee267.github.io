Learning when measurements are scarce

Environmental models often serve the regions with the least monitoring infrastructure. This work asks how graph structure, physical relationships, domain knowledge, and efficient sensing mechanisms can make useful predictions without assuming dense, uniform data.

Evaluation across place and time

The central evaluation question is not merely whether a model fits historical observations, but whether it transfers to places with limited monitoring and to seasons it has not seen. GraPhy tests structure-aware imputation under constrained coverage; FROST evaluates unseen California stations, multiple seasons, and forecast horizons from 3 to 24 hours.

Why it matters

Fine-grained air-quality and frost information supports public health, agricultural decisions, and communities that cannot rely on dense sensor networks. Physics-guided models and compact predictors offer a path toward systems that are more accurate, deployable, and equitable.
