Research area 03

Adaptive & embedded sensing

Designing the physical path that information takes—from structures that shape signals to embedded hardware, constrained planning, and learning architectures informed by circuits.

On this page

Better inference can begin before the sensor

Instead of treating the physical environment as a fixed source of noisy data, this research co-designs structures, mechanisms, circuits, algorithms, and models. Metamaterials can encode direction, kinetic attachments can encode object identity, and reconfigurable infrastructure can move sensors toward better observations.

Selected systems

UbiSense ’26

GEM

A hybrid gear matrix embedded in the environment so infrastructure-mounted sensors can reposition without requiring a wearable or a free-roaming robot.

  • Modified A-star planning
  • Transfer and Swap operations
  • Parity and collision constraints

ACM SenSys ’24

OMSense

A Miura-Ori metasurface that physically isolates pressure-sensing units, paired with a circuit-guided CNN that removes multiplexing ghost activations.

Read the paper ↗
Best Poster · SenSys ’23

LEVO

Configurable LEGO-based metamaterial arrays encode source direction into vibration modes observable at a single sensing point.

Read the paper ↗
ACM HotMobile ’24

KAP

A passive 3D-printed mechanism changes the physical signature of medication interactions so a remote ambient sensor can identify the bottle.

My contributions

For GEM, I designed, proved, and implemented the constrained path-finding algorithms. For OMSense, I designed, implemented, and evaluated the circuit-guided CNN. For KAP, I designed and implemented the kinetic-aware time-frequency feature pipeline. For LEVO, I conducted experiments and signal analysis.

Technical toolkit

Physical prototyping, kinetic mechanisms, origami structures, metamaterials, gear matrices, ESP32-class sensor nodes, ADC and geophone acquisition, piezoelectric sensors, circuit-guided learning, STFT analysis, event detection, and constrained path finding.
