Health & activity sensing

Privacy-conscious sensing for remote care, medication interactions, dental function, and daily activity—designed around the realities of users, homes, and clinical context.

Care technology has to work where people live

Remote-care systems fail when installation is fragile, labels are unavailable, or the sensing burden falls on the person being monitored. I build ambient and wearable systems that preserve existing routines while making deployment and validation more interactive.

This work joins vibration sensing, embedded acquisition, activity recognition, LLM-guided configuration, and human-centered validation.

From model to deployment

The deployment stack samples structural vibration at approximately 6.8–7 kHz, streams data over low-overhead UDP, performs packet-level recovery, tunnels securely through WireGuard, and uses a local edge hub. The work treats networking stability, sensor disturbance, privacy, labeling, and usability as research variables rather than afterthoughts.

Design principles

Preserve familiar objects and routines. Prefer ambient sensing when wearables add burden. Make system decisions legible to caregivers and researchers. Evaluate performance across placement, users, and real homes—not only curated laboratory data.
